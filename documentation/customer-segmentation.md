# Customer & Sales Analytics — Customer Segmentation Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This document describes the customer segmentation methodology used in the Customer & Sales Analytics project.

The objective of the segmentation model is to move beyond basic customer counts and identify meaningful differences in customer behaviour using three dimensions:

- Recency
- Frequency
- Value

The approach is inspired by RFM-style customer analysis but uses Revenue as the monetary/value component and is referred to throughout this project as **RFV — Recency, Frequency and Value**.

The segmentation is designed for analytical exploration rather than automated marketing activation or predictive churn modelling.


---

# 2. Analytical Scope

The segmentation uses customer purchasing behaviour across the complete observed transactional period:

**1 January 2022 – 31 December 2025**

It is therefore a **static lifetime segmentation** based on the available dataset.

A customer's segment describes their overall observed behaviour across this period.

It does not represent the segment to which the customer belonged at every historical point in time.


---

# 3. Segmentation Population

The primary segmentation population consists of customers with at least one completed purchase.

Validated purchasing-customer population:

**17,773 customers**

The full customer dimension contains:

**20,000 registered customers**

Customers without a completed purchase are retained in the model but are excluded from purchasing-customer quartile calculations.

They are classified separately as:

`No Completed Purchase`


---

# 4. Why RFV Was Used

A single metric such as total revenue does not provide enough information to describe customer behaviour.

For example, two customers may have generated the same lifetime revenue while displaying very different purchasing patterns.

One may:

- purchase frequently;
- have purchased recently;
- maintain consistent activity.

Another may:

- have made only a few large purchases;
- not have purchased recently.

RFV combines multiple behavioural dimensions to provide a richer customer profile.


---

# 5. Recency

Recency measures how recently a customer made their last observed completed purchase.

The underlying customer-level metric is:

`Days Since Last Purchase`

A lower value indicates more recent activity.


## Reference Date

Recency is calculated relative to the fixed analytical endpoint:

**31 December 2025**

rather than using:

`TODAY()`


## Reason

The dataset ends on 31 December 2025.

Using the current system date would cause customer recency classifications to change over time even though the underlying dataset remains unchanged.

A fixed reference date makes the portfolio results reproducible.


---

# 6. Frequency

Frequency represents how often a customer purchased during the complete observed period.

The underlying metric is:

`Lifetime Orders`

Orders are counted using distinct `OrderID` values because the sales fact table operates at order-line grain.

Higher Lifetime Orders indicates greater purchasing frequency.


---

# 7. Value

Value represents the customer's commercial contribution.

The underlying metric is:

`Lifetime Revenue`

Lifetime Revenue represents completed-sale net revenue generated by the customer across the available 2022–2025 transaction history.

Higher Lifetime Revenue indicates greater observed customer value.


---

# 8. Quartile-Based Scoring

Recency, Frequency and Value are converted into scores from:

**1 to 4**

The scores are derived from the distribution of the purchasing-customer population.

This produces data-driven segmentation rather than relying on arbitrary fixed thresholds.


## Score Interpretation

| Score | General Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 4 | Strongest |
| 3 | Above-middle |
| 2 | Below-middle |
| 1 | Weakest |

The interpretation differs slightly for Recency because lower Days Since Last Purchase represents stronger behaviour.


---

# 9. Value Scoring

Customers are ranked using:

`Lifetime Revenue`

Higher revenue receives a higher Value Score.

The resulting score ranges from:

`1` — lowest customer value

to

`4` — highest customer value.


## Value Segments

| Value Score | Segment |
|---:|---|
| 4 | Premium Value |
| 3 | High Value |
| 2 | Medium Value |
| 1 | Low Value |

Customers without completed purchases are handled separately.


---

# 10. Frequency Scoring

Customers are ranked using:

`Lifetime Orders`

Higher purchase frequency receives a higher Frequency Score.


## Frequency Segments

The reporting categories are ordered:

1. Very High Frequency
2. High Frequency
3. Moderate Frequency
4. Low Frequency

The ordering is controlled using a dedicated sort column rather than alphabetical sorting.


---

# 11. Recency Scoring

Recency requires reversed scoring.

Unlike Revenue and Orders, a **smaller** Days Since Last Purchase value represents stronger behaviour.

Therefore:

- most recent customers receive higher Recency Scores;
- least recent customers receive lower Recency Scores.


## Recency Segments

The reporting categories are:

1. Recently Active
2. Moderately Recent
3. Less Recent
4. Least Recent

This provides a business-friendly interpretation of the underlying recency scores.


---

# 12. Customer Behaviour Score

The individual RFV scores are also available together as a customer behaviour score.

The underlying components are:

- Recency Score
- Frequency Score
- Value Score

These scores provide the inputs for the final behavioural segmentation rules.


---

# 13. Final Customer Segments

The model classifies purchasing customers into six primary behavioural segments:

1. High-Value Loyal
2. Valuable Regular
3. Recent Developing
4. Core Customer
5. High-Value At Risk
6. Low Engagement

A seventh classification is retained for:

7. No Completed Purchase


---

# 14. High-Value Loyal

Rule:

- Recency Score >= 3
- Frequency Score >= 3
- Value Score >= 3

These customers combine:

- relatively recent purchasing;
- relatively high purchase frequency;
- relatively high commercial value.

This is the strongest combined RFV profile in the segmentation framework.


---

# 15. Recent Developing

Rule:

- Recency Score >= 3
- Frequency Score <= 2

These customers have purchased relatively recently but have not yet developed high observed purchase frequency.

They may include newer or less-established purchasing relationships.

The segment name describes observed behaviour only and does not predict future development.


---

# 16. High-Value At Risk

Rule:

- Recency Score <= 2
- Frequency Score >= 3
- Value Score >= 3

These customers have historically demonstrated:

- relatively strong value;
- relatively strong purchase frequency;

but weaker recent purchasing activity.

The term `At Risk` refers specifically to reduced recency within the segmentation framework.

It should not be interpreted as confirmed churn.


---

# 17. Low Engagement

Rule:

- Recency Score <= 2
- Frequency Score <= 2

These customers combine:

- weaker recent activity;
- lower observed purchasing frequency.

This classification describes engagement with completed purchasing activity and does not establish the reason for the behaviour.


---

# 18. Valuable Regular

After the more specific RFV rules are evaluated, remaining customers with:

`Value Score >= 3`

are classified as:

`Valuable Regular`

These customers demonstrate relatively strong customer value but do not meet the combined conditions required for High-Value Loyal or High-Value At Risk.


---

# 19. Core Customer

Remaining purchasing customers that do not meet the preceding specialist segment rules are classified as:

`Core Customer`

This provides a complete mutually exclusive segmentation of the purchasing population.


---

# 20. No Completed Purchase

Registered customers with:

`Lifetime Orders = 0`

are classified separately as:

`No Completed Purchase`

These customers remain valid members of `DimCustomer` but do not contribute to completed-sales customer segmentation.

They are excluded from RFV quartile thresholds to prevent non-purchasers from distorting the purchasing-customer distribution.


---

# 21. Rule Evaluation Order

The segmentation rules are evaluated in a controlled sequence.

The logic is:

1. No Completed Purchase
2. High-Value Loyal
3. Recent Developing
4. High-Value At Risk
5. Low Engagement
6. Valuable Regular
7. Core Customer

The sequence matters because a customer may satisfy elements of more than one broad behavioural condition.

More specific segment rules are therefore evaluated before fallback classifications.


---

# 22. Why Arbitrary Monetary Thresholds Were Avoided

The segmentation does not use rules such as:

- Revenue above £5,000 = Premium
- More than 10 orders = Loyal
- Last purchase within 30 days = Recent

Such thresholds would require an external business justification.

Instead, the project derives customer tiers from the observed customer distribution.


## Benefit

This makes the segmentation:

- data-driven;
- reproducible;
- appropriate to the scale of the dataset;
- less dependent on unsupported assumptions.


## Limitation

Quartile-based segmentation is relative.

A Premium Value customer is high value relative to other customers in this dataset.

It does not imply that the same revenue threshold would define Premium customers in another business.


---

# 23. Value Segment Validation

The quartile-based Value segmentation produced:

| Value Segment | Customers |
|---|---:|
| Premium Value | 4,443 |
| High Value | 4,443 |
| Medium Value | 4,443 |
| Low Value | 4,444 |
| **Total** | **17,773** |

This near-even customer distribution is expected because the groups are derived from quartile thresholds.


---

# 24. Revenue Concentration

Although the Value segments contain approximately equal numbers of purchasing customers, their revenue contribution is highly unequal.

Validated results:

| Value Segment | Approx. Revenue | Approx. Revenue Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Premium Value | £29.30M | 63.7% |
| High Value | £10.13M | 22.0% |
| Medium Value | £4.91M | 10.7% |
| Low Value | £1.68M | 3.6% |

Total Revenue:

**£46.03M**


## Interpretation

Premium Value customers represent approximately:

**25% of purchasing customers**

while contributing approximately:

**63.7% of total revenue**

This demonstrates substantial revenue concentration among the highest-value customer quartile.


---

# 25. Recency and Value Pattern

The Recency × Value analysis shows that Premium Value customers are more concentrated in stronger recency groups than lower-value customers.

Validated customer counts include:

| Recency Group | High | Low | Medium | Premium |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Recently Active | 1,090 | 623 | 790 | 2,014 |
| Moderately Recent | 1,190 | 857 | 1,012 | 1,320 |
| Less Recent | 1,240 | 1,238 | 1,251 | 715 |
| Least Recent | 923 | 1,726 | 1,390 | 394 |

This pattern shows an association between stronger observed customer value and more recent purchasing activity.

It does not establish that recency causes higher customer value.


---

# 26. Frequency and Value Pattern

The Frequency × Value analysis also demonstrates a strong relationship between purchase frequency and customer value.

Premium Value customers are heavily represented among the highest-frequency customers, while Low Value customers are more concentrated among lower-frequency customers.

This supports the use of multiple behavioural dimensions rather than analysing customer revenue in isolation.

Again, this represents association within the observed dataset rather than causal evidence.


---

# 27. Segment Sorting

Power BI report categories use dedicated numerical sort columns.

Examples include:

- Value Segment Sort
- Frequency Segment Sort
- Recency Segment Sort
- Customer Segment Sort

These ensure that categories appear in analytical rather than alphabetical order.


## Value Order

1. Premium Value
2. High Value
3. Medium Value
4. Low Value


## Frequency Order

1. Very High Frequency
2. High Frequency
3. Moderate Frequency
4. Low Frequency


## Recency Order

1. Recently Active
2. Moderately Recent
3. Less Recent
4. Least Recent


## Customer Segment Order

1. High-Value Loyal
2. Valuable Regular
3. Recent Developing
4. Core Customer
5. High-Value At Risk
6. Low Engagement
7. No Completed Purchase


---

# 28. Avoiding Circular Dependencies

The sort columns do not simply reference the text classification they are used to sort.

For example, `Customer Segment Sort` repeats the underlying segmentation conditions rather than evaluating:

`DimCustomer[Customer Segment]`

directly.

This avoids a circular dependency between:

`Customer Segment`

and:

`Customer Segment Sort`

when Power BI's `Sort by Column` configuration is applied.


---

# 29. Share-of-Total Filter Context

Power BI may include a category's associated sort field within visual grouping/filter context.

For this reason, segmentation share measures remove both:

- the visible segmentation field;
- its associated sort field;

when calculating the denominator.

For example, Value Segment Revenue Share removes filters from both:

`Customer Value Segment`

and:

`Value Segment Sort`

This ensures that the denominator represents the intended total rather than remaining restricted by a hidden sort-column filter.


---

# 30. Static Segmentation Limitation

The RFV segmentation is calculated using the complete observed 2022–2025 history.

Therefore, applying a Year filter to sales does not reconstruct what the customer's RFV segment would have been at the end of that historical year.

For example:

A customer classified as `Premium Value` based on their complete 2022–2025 activity should not automatically be described as Premium Value in 2022.


## Reporting Decision

The dedicated Customer Segmentation report page therefore does not expose a Year slicer as though the segmentation were historical.

This avoids implying point-in-time segmentation functionality that the model does not provide.


---

# 31. Segmentation vs Customer Trends

Static segmentation and dynamic customer movement serve different analytical purposes.

## Segmentation

Answers:

> What type of customer is this based on complete observed purchasing behaviour?


## Customer Trends

Answers:

> How is this customer's purchasing activity changing between the selected period and the equivalent previous period?

A High-Value Loyal customer can therefore still be classified dynamically as:

- Growing;
- Stable;
- Declining;

depending on the selected comparison period.

The two frameworks complement rather than replace one another.


---

# 32. Interpretation Guidelines

The segmentation should be interpreted carefully.

### High-Value Loyal

Indicates a strong combination of recency, frequency and value within the dataset.

### High-Value At Risk

Indicates historically strong frequency/value with weaker recency.

It does not prove that the customer will churn.

### Recent Developing

Indicates stronger recency but lower observed frequency.

It does not guarantee future growth.

### Low Engagement

Indicates weaker recency and frequency.

It does not identify the cause.

### Premium Value

Indicates the highest relative Lifetime Revenue quartile.

It is not an externally defined monetary customer tier.


---

# 33. Business Use

Within a real retail environment, this segmentation framework could support activities such as:

- prioritising customer analysis;
- identifying commercially important customer groups;
- comparing product behaviour across customer tiers;
- analysing high-value customers with weakening activity;
- understanding the concentration of revenue;
- supporting targeted follow-up analysis.

Any operational marketing or retention action would require additional business context, governance and customer-level validation beyond the descriptive analytics presented here.


---

# 34. Outcome

The RFV segmentation extends the analytical model from:

**Who purchased?**

to:

**How valuable are they, how frequently do they purchase, and how recently have they been active?**

The resulting framework provides a reproducible and data-driven method for exploring customer differences while clearly separating observed behaviour from unsupported predictions or causal claims.
